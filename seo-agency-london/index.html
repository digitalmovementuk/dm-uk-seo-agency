01 01 — The market

London is thirty-three markets, not one

Digital Movement Marketing Ltd is registered at 128 City Road, London EC1V 2NX — the stretch between Old Street and Angel, on the Islington side of the Shoreditch boundary and a short walk from the Square Mile. That corner of the capital happens to illustrate the problem London businesses face in search. Within a mile of the door you cross into Hackney and Tower Hamlets, pass into the City of London, and move through four or five neighbourhoods that buyers name completely differently when they type. None of it is simply “London” to the person searching.

Greater London is thirty-two boroughs plus the City of London, spread over roughly 1,570 square kilometres. Google's map results are weighted heavily by how close a business sits to the person searching, and in a city this dense that weighting bites hard. A firm in Croydon will almost never appear in the three-business map result shown to someone standing in Camden, however good its website is. This is the misunderstanding we correct most often: a business asks why it does not rank “in London”, when in truth it ranks perfectly well within three or four miles of its own front door and nowhere else.

So the honest first question is not how to rank in London. It is which parts of London you can actually serve, and whether the money is in nearby buyers at all. For a dental practice in Islington or a plumber covering the south-west, proximity is everything and the work is local. For a City law firm, a Mayfair wealth manager or a Shoreditch software company, most buyers are not searching by neighbourhood — they are searching by specialism, and an address counts for very little. We establish which of the two you are before recommending anything, and plenty of London businesses turn out to be both.

02 02 — Sectors

How search behaves in London's biggest sectors

The capital's economy is concentrated in a handful of high-value services, and buyers in each of them behave differently the moment they open a search bar.

01

Financial & professional services

The Square Mile and Canary Wharf run on referral and reputation, so search is usually a checking step rather than a discovery one. A prospect already has your name; they look you up, read the site and decide whether you seem credible. That makes named-company results, team biographies, sector pages and third-party mentions worth more than any local ranking. The City has only a few thousand residents but hundreds of thousands of daytime workers, so demand there is weekday, commuter-shaped and business-to-business.

02

Legal

London's legal market splits in two. Around Chancery Lane, Holborn and the Inns of Court the work is high-value and researched at length — buyers compare practice areas, read about outcomes and check credentials before they call. Out in the boroughs the same profession competes on immediate need: conveyancing, family, immigration and employment, searched by area and won or lost in the map result. A firm covering both needs two genuinely different pieces of work, not one compromise.

03

Property, lettings & construction

Property is the most tightly area-bound category in the capital. Buyers search by neighbourhood, postcode district and sometimes station, and they will not accept a business two boroughs away. For agents and contractors that means a page for each area you genuinely operate in, written about that area, with branch coverage stated plainly. It also means being straight about the Congestion Charge and ULEZ: trades that price central jobs differently, or decline them, should say so on the page rather than field the calls.

04

Hospitality, hotels & food

Restaurants, bars, hotels and venues live on the map result, the reviews and the photographs attached to the Google Business Profile. Search here is immediate — people are nearby and deciding within the hour — and it peaks around commuter and visitor patterns rather than office hours. Volume is high and competition is dense, so small operational details do more for bookings than most content work: accurate opening hours, a working reservation link, current menus and recent photos.

05

Private healthcare & clinics

Harley Street and the wider private clinic sector sit between the two models. The specialism is searched nationally and internationally, while the practical enquiry is often made by someone close by. These practices usually need a strong page for each treatment, written to answer what the patient is actually worried about, plus a complete and verified profile so the clinic appears when someone searches nearby. Regulated claims need care, and we keep the copy inside what can be evidenced.

06

Technology & creative

Around Old Street, Shoreditch and out towards King's Cross, buyers research a considered purchase over weeks and very little of it is local. What works is depth on the problem you solve, honest pricing signals, proof a buyer can check, and being findable in AI assistants as well as Google — which is why AI search optimisation matters more to these companies than a map ranking ever will.

03 03 — Geography

The geography that decides your targeting

Naming an area correctly matters more in London than anywhere else in Britain, because the names people use are not administrative units. The City of London is its own local authority and “the City” means something precise to the people who work there. Westminster covers the West End, Mayfair, Soho and Victoria — four markets no buyer would treat as one. Shoreditch is not a borough at all: it straddles Hackney, Islington and Tower Hamlets, which is exactly why a business there should describe itself by neighbourhood rather than council area.

Below that sits a second layer of names people actually type — postcode districts, high streets and Tube stations. “Near Liverpool Street”, “off Clapham High Street”, SE1, W1: all of these are live search behaviour, and they tell you more about where a business can compete than any borough boundary. Where a firm has one address, we build one strong page for it and let the Google Business Profile do the local work. Where it genuinely operates across several areas — separate branches, real coverage, staff who travel there — we build a page per area, each written about that area, and we decline the rest.

That last point is where most agencies go wrong and where Google is least forgiving. Publishing thirty near-identical pages with the borough name swapped is the exact pattern named in its spam policy, and it puts the whole site at risk for very little upside. If you can honestly write four hundred words about working in an area — the sort of jobs you take there, the buildings, the travel, the constraints — it deserves a page of its own. If you cannot, it does not.

04 04 — Priorities

When local work beats national work

Local search is the right priority when your buyers are nearby, when the job needs someone to turn up, and when a decision gets made in hours rather than weeks. Trades, clinics, restaurants, gyms, garages, veterinary practices, opticians, nurseries — for all of these the three-business map result above the normal listings carries a large share of the demand. The work that wins it is unglamorous: a complete and verified Google Business Profile, business details that match everywhere they appear, a steady flow of genuine reviews, and a site that loads fast and puts the phone number where a thumb can reach it.

National work is the right priority when buyers compare before they commit, when the contract is worth thousands rather than hundreds, and when the person searching does not care where you sit. That covers most professional services, most software, and a good deal of specialist healthcare. Here the budget goes into depth — pages that answer the question better than the alternatives, proof a buyer can verify, and the authority that lets a site compete for harder terms. The full SEO programme we run covers both, and the mix is set by whichever will produce enquiries first.

Most London businesses need some of each, sequenced rather than run at once. We start with whatever produces the earliest enquiries, because early results fund the patience the harder work needs. If the honest answer is that paid search will bring customers faster while the organic work builds, we will say so. And if you already get enough enquiries but lose them after they arrive, a CRM that catches and chases every one is a better place to spend than search.

06 06 — Questions

Fair questions, straight answers

The questions London owners and marketing teams actually ask before a first call.

Can you rank my business across the whole of London?

Almost certainly not, and any agency promising it is selling you something. Google's map results are weighted heavily by how close a business sits to the person searching, and Greater London covers thirty-two boroughs plus the City of London. A single address ranks reliably within a few miles of itself. What we can do is win the areas you can genuinely reach, and separately compete for the sector and service searches where your address does not matter.

Should I build a page for every London borough?

No. Publishing thirty near-identical pages with the borough name swapped is the exact pattern Google's spam policy names, and it risks the whole site for very little gain. Build a page for an area only when you can write something true and specific about working there — the jobs you take, the buildings, the travel, the constraints. If you cannot fill it honestly, the area does not need a page.

Do I need a London address to rank in London?

For map results, yes in practice: Google verifies a real address or a defined service area, and proximity drives what appears. For ordinary organic results, no. A business anywhere in the country can rank for London sector searches on the strength of its content and authority. Which of the two matters depends entirely on whether your buyers need you nearby.

Is local SEO or national SEO right for a London business?

It depends on how your buyers decide. If the job needs someone to turn up and the decision is made in hours — trades, clinics, restaurants, garages — local work and the map result carry the demand. If buyers compare over weeks and do not care where you sit, which covers most professional services and software, the money is in depth, proof and authority on sector searches.
