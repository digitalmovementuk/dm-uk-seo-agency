01 01 — The region

Ten towns wearing one name

Greater Manchester is not a city with suburbs. It is ten metropolitan boroughs — Manchester, Salford, Bolton, Bury, Oldham, Rochdale, Stockport, Tameside, Trafford and Wigan — each with its own council, its own town centre and, as far as search is concerned, its own market. Wigan sits closer to Liverpool than to Stockport. Bolton is a fifteen-mile drive from Deansgate. People from those towns say where they are from, and they type it into Google the same way.

That single fact shapes the work more than anything else about the region. Google's map results are weighted heavily by how close a business sits to the person searching, so a city-centre address will not appear in the three-business result someone sees standing in Bolton town centre. If your customers come from more than one borough, a plan built entirely around the word “Manchester” will quietly miss most of your demand.

The reverse trap is just as common. Businesses that genuinely serve one town spend on the city term because it looks bigger, then find the enquiries come from people too far away to buy. We would rather map the towns you actually sell to, check what people there search for, and build one real page for each — never a template with the town name swapped in, which is the pattern Google's spam policy names and which does more harm than good.

One more piece of local geography that businesses get wrong in their own copy: MediaCityUK is in Salford, not Manchester, and so is most of the broadcast sector attached to it. The Trafford Centre and Old Trafford are in Trafford. Getting this right on your own site is a small signal that you know the area. Getting it wrong in front of a local buyer is a small thing they notice.

02 02 — Sectors

The sectors that shape search here

Four parts of the regional economy dominate what we are asked to work on, and buyers in each behave very differently.

01

Media, broadcast & production

MediaCityUK at Salford Quays anchors a broadcast and production cluster, and the businesses around it — studios, post houses, kit hire, freelancers, agencies — sell to commissioners and producers rather than to the public. Search for them is a credibility check and a specialism search, never a local one. Credits, showreels and clearly defined services do more than any town ranking, and being visible to AI assistants matters because a researcher building a shortlist now often asks one before asking around.

02

Technology & digital

Manchester holds one of the larger technology clusters outside London, and its buyers behave like buyers of any considered purchase: they research for weeks, compare openly, and rarely care where the supplier sits. That work is national and sector-based, built on depth, proof and honest pricing signals. Getting cited in AI answers is turning into a real channel for these companies, because the shortlist an assistant produces is increasingly the first one a buyer sees.

03

Legal, accountancy & professional services

Spinningfields and the city core hold a dense professional services market, and it splits the way it does everywhere: corporate work researched at length, and everyday consumer work — conveyancing, wills, family, tax returns — decided quickly and close to home. The second kind is won in the town map results, which is precisely why a Manchester-only plan fails a firm whose clients are in Bury, Bolton and Stockport.

04

Manufacturing, logistics & trades

Away from the centre the regional economy still makes things and moves them, alongside the trades serving homes and sites across ten boroughs. For manufacturers and specialist suppliers the valuable searches are technical and national — a material, a process, a tolerance — and the town name barely features. For trades it is the reverse: the map result and the reviews decide it, borough by borough, and coverage claims need to match where the vans genuinely go.
