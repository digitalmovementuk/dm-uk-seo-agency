This notice was prepared on 28 July 2026 and describes how this website handles enquiry details. It should be reviewed and approved by Digital Movement UK before being relied upon.

This notice explains what happens to your details when you contact Digital Movement UK through this website. It is written to be read by the people it affects rather than by lawyers, and it is deliberately specific, because this site collects very little and stores none of it.

It covers the enquiry forms on this website and the emails they produce. It does not cover what we do with client data once you engage us for work — that is set out in the agreement we would sign with you — and it does not cover other companies' websites that we link to.

Digital Movement Marketing Ltd is subject to the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018. Our supervisory authority is the Information Commissioner's Office.

03 03 — The mechanism

How these forms actually work

This matters more than it sounds, because it changes who holds your data and when. The forms on this site do not submit to a web server. They hand your answers to your own email program.

When you press the send button, the page opens a new email on your device, addressed to office@digitalmovement.uk, with your answers written into the body of the message as plain text. You then send it yourself, from your own email account. Three consequences follow, and we would rather you knew all three.

Nothing is stored on this website

There is no database behind these forms, no lead-capture platform, no third-party form processor and no marketing automation. Your details never sit on this website's server, because they never arrive there.

Nothing reaches us until you send it

If the draft email opens and you close it, change your mind, or delete it, we never see anything. The form itself has told us nothing.

It is an ordinary email, with the usual properties

The message travels from your email account, through your email provider and ours, to our inbox. A copy stays in your own Sent folder. Your name and email address appear on it because it is genuinely your message, sent by you. Ordinary email is not encrypted end to end, so please do not use an enquiry form for anything sensitive — health details, financial details, or anything to do with a legal or criminal matter.

If nothing happens when you press send

This method depends on your device having an email program set up. On some phones and shared or locked-down computers it will not, and the button will appear to do nothing. If that happens, email office@digitalmovement.uk directly or call 0203 815 7992. Nothing was sent, and nothing was kept.

In practiceForm → your email app → you press send → our inbox.

No web database. No form processor. No copy on this server.
04 04 — Why, and on what basis

What we use it for, and the lawful basis

We use the details you send for one thing: reading and answering your enquiry, and having whatever conversation follows from it. If that conversation heads towards working together, the same details are used to prepare a proposal or a quote.

Our lawful basis

UK GDPR requires a lawful basis for every use of personal data. Depending on why you contacted us, one of two applies.

  • →Steps taken at your request before entering into a contract — Article 6(1)(b). If your enquiry is about hiring us, replying to it is one of the steps you asked us to take.
  • →Our legitimate interests — Article 6(1)(f). Where the enquiry is not about becoming a client — a general question, a press enquiry, a supplier introduction — our legitimate interest is simply being able to answer people who write to us. You contacted us and would expect a reply, we use your details for nothing else, and we do not consider this to override your rights or interests. If you disagree, you can object; see section 07.

About the tick box

The tick box on each form confirms that you are happy for us to use those details to reply to you. It is not, on its own, the whole of our legal basis — the two above are. We spell this out because "consent" has a narrow, specific meaning in data protection law, and treating a tick box as if it were doing more work than it is would be misleading.

Where we do rely on consent for something separate — for example if you ask us to send you a document or add you to something — you can withdraw it at any time by emailing office@digitalmovement.uk. Withdrawing consent does not make what we did beforehand unlawful; it stops us going any further.

What we do not do with it

We do not add enquiries to a mailing list. We do not use your details to advertise to you, we do not build a profile of you, and we do not make any decision about you by automated means.

05 05 — How long

How long we keep it

Your enquiry arrives as an email and lives in our business inbox, so it lasts as long as that email does. We keep it for as long as it is needed for the purpose it was sent for: answering you, and any conversation that comes out of that.

If you go on to become a client, records connected to the work — agreements, invoices and correspondence about the engagement — are kept for as long as we need them to run the engagement and to meet our legal, tax and accounting obligations. Those periods are set by UK law rather than by us.

We have not fixed a set number of months for enquiry correspondence that goes nowhere, and we would rather say that plainly than publish a figure we do not actually apply. If you would like your enquiry and our reply deleted, ask us and we will delete them, unless there is something we are legally required to keep — in which case we will tell you what, and why.

07 07 — Cookies

Cookies, analytics and what this site stores

This website sets no cookies. We checked the code rather than assuming it, and there is nothing there.

There is no Google Analytics on this site, no Google Tag Manager, no Meta or LinkedIn pixel, no Microsoft or Google advertising tag, no session-recording or heatmap tool and no A/B testing script. The only JavaScript file the pages load is our own /assets/site.js, which runs the mobile menu, the scroll animations and the number counters. It sets no cookies and writes nothing to your device.

That is why you have not been shown a cookie banner. There is nothing on these pages to consent to. Three things are still worth saying, because they are true and most privacy notices leave them out.

The fonts come from Google

The pages use three typefaces served by Google Fonts, from fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com. Your browser fetches them directly from Google, which means Google receives your IP address and the fact that a page on this site was loaded. The Google Fonts web API does not set cookies, but that request still happens, and Google is a company based in the United States. We are reviewing whether to serve these font files from our own server so the request stops happening at all.

One page stores something in your browser

One page on this site — the Bupa SEO teardown, at /resources/bupa-seo-teardown/ — works differently from the rest. Its download form goes through a third-party form service (FormSubmit, formsubmit.co) rather than through your email program, and it saves two items in your browser's local storage: a flag recording that you unlocked the report, and a copy of the name, email address and phone number you entered. Local storage sits on your own device; you can clear it at any time through your browser settings, usually under "clear site data". That page is under review, and this notice will be updated when it changes.

The server keeps ordinary logs

Like any website, the server that delivers these pages may keep standard access logs — the technical record of an IP address, a browser type and which file was requested. Those are created by our hosting provider as part of running and securing the service, not by us, and we do not use them to identify or follow individual visitors. We are confirming what our host retains and for how long, and this notice will say so.

Links to other sites

The site links out to Google, to WhatsApp and to a small number of other sites. Those links do nothing at all until you click one. Once you do, you are on somebody else's site, under their privacy policy rather than ours.

How to use any of them

Email office@digitalmovement.uk, or write to Digital Movement Marketing Ltd, 128 City Road, London, EC1V 2NX, United Kingdom. You do not need any particular form of words; telling us plainly what you want is enough.
