This notice was prepared on 28 July 2026 and describes how this website handles enquiry details. It should be reviewed and approved by Digital Movement UK before being relied upon.

This notice explains what happens to your details when you contact Digital Movement UK through this website. It is written to be read by the people it affects rather than by lawyers, and it is deliberately specific about the parts most notices gloss over: which company handles your enquiry on the way to us, and what is recorded alongside what you typed.

It covers the enquiry forms on this website and the emails they produce. It does not cover what we do with client data once you engage us for work — that is set out in the agreement we would sign with you — and it does not cover other companies' websites that we link to.

Digital Movement Marketing Ltd is subject to the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018. Our supervisory authority is the Information Commissioner's Office.

03 03 — The mechanism

How these forms actually work

Your answers go to a third-party service, which turns them into an email to us. That company sits between you and our inbox, so you should know it is there and what it gets.

When you press the send button, the page checks your answers and then sends them to FormSubmit (formsubmit.co), a hosted service that converts a website form into an email. FormSubmit delivers that email to office@digitalmovement.uk. You stay on the page, and a confirmation appears where the button was; you are not taken anywhere.

What FormSubmit receives

Everything set out in section 02: the answers you typed, whether you ticked the consent box, the consent wording and the timestamp, the page address, which form you used and how long the page had been open — together with your IP address, which it records itself at the moment the submission arrives.

FormSubmit is a processor, and here is what we have not yet confirmed

FormSubmit is not us. It is a separate company that we do not own or control, acting as a processor: it handles your submission on our instructions, for the single purpose of delivering it to our inbox.

We are not going to imply that more of this is settled than actually is. At the time of writing we have not confirmed which country FormSubmit's servers are in, whether it keeps a copy of submissions after delivering them and for how long, or whether a written data processing agreement and an appropriate international transfer safeguard are in place between us. Those are being established, and this notice will be updated with the answers. We would rather tell you now than after the fact.

If you would prefer your details did not pass through a third-party service, you do not have to use the form. Email office@digitalmovement.uk or call 0203 815 7992 and you reach us directly, with nobody in between.

If the send fails

Nothing is lost. Your answers stay in the form, and a message offers you our phone number and a link that opens a message in your own email program with your details already written into it. If you take that route your enquiry travels as ordinary email, and FormSubmit is not involved at all.

Older browsers, and no JavaScript

If your browser cannot use the modern method the page prefers, it posts the form straight to FormSubmit itself and you land on our thank-you page at /thank-you/. If JavaScript is switched off altogether, the form falls back to its original behaviour: it opens a new message in your own email program, addressed to us, and nothing reaches anyone until you press send yourself.

Please keep sensitive detail out of it

An enquiry form, and the email it becomes, is not a secure channel, and it now passes through a third party as well. Please do not use one for anything sensitive — health details, financial details, or anything to do with a legal or criminal matter. Call us instead and we will find a better way to take it.

04 04 — Why, and on what basis

What we use it for, and the lawful basis

We use the details you send for one thing: reading and answering your enquiry, and having whatever conversation follows from it. If that conversation heads towards working together, the same details are used to prepare a proposal or a quote.

Our lawful basis

UK GDPR requires a lawful basis for every use of personal data. Depending on why you contacted us, one of two applies.

  • →Steps taken at your request before entering into a contract — Article 6(1)(b). If your enquiry is about hiring us, replying to it is one of the steps you asked us to take.
  • →Our legitimate interests — Article 6(1)(f). Where the enquiry is not about becoming a client — a general question, a press enquiry, a supplier introduction — our legitimate interest is simply being able to answer people who write to us. You contacted us and would expect a reply, we use your details for nothing else, and we do not consider this to override your rights or interests. If you disagree, you can object; see section 08.

About the tick box, and the record we keep of it

The tick box on each form confirms that you are happy for us to use those details to reply to you. It is not, on its own, the whole of our legal basis — the two above are. We spell this out because "consent" has a narrow, specific meaning in data protection law, and treating a tick box as if it were doing more work than it is would be misleading.

We do keep a record of it, described in section 02: the exact words that were on your screen, and when you sent the form. That record exists so that if you ever ask what you agreed to, you get the actual sentence you read rather than somebody's recollection of it. We rely on our legitimate interest in being able to show that we handled your details properly.

Where we rely on consent for something separate — if you ask us to send you a document, or to add you to something — you can withdraw it at any time by emailing office@digitalmovement.uk. Withdrawing consent does not make what we did beforehand unlawful; it stops us going any further.

What we do not do with it

We do not add enquiries to a mailing list. We do not use your details to advertise to you, we do not build a profile of you, and we do not make any decision about you by automated means.

05 05 — How long

How long we keep it

Your enquiry arrives as an email and lives in our business inbox, so it lasts as long as that email does. We keep it for as long as it is needed for the purpose it was sent for: answering you, and any conversation that comes out of that.

If you go on to become a client, records connected to the work — agreements, invoices and correspondence about the engagement — are kept for as long as we need them to run the engagement and to meet our legal, tax and accounting obligations. Those periods are set by UK law rather than by us.

We have not fixed a set number of months for enquiry correspondence that goes nowhere, and we would rather say that plainly than publish a figure we do not actually apply. If you would like your enquiry and our reply deleted, ask us and we will delete them, unless there is something we are legally required to keep — in which case we will tell you what, and why.

One honest gap: we do not yet know whether FormSubmit keeps its own copy of a submission after it has emailed it to us, or for how long. We are asking, and this section will say so once we have the answer. A deletion request to us covers our own records, and we will pass it on as well.

Outside the United Kingdom

Digital Movement works across the United Kingdom, Australia and New Zealand. Depending on who is best placed to help, an enquiry sent to office@digitalmovement.uk may be read by a colleague outside the UK.

Under UK data protection law, New Zealand is covered by UK adequacy regulations: the UK Government recognises that it offers an equivalent standard of protection, so personal data can move there without additional safeguards. Australia is not covered by adequacy regulations. Personal data going there needs a specific safeguard — such as the UK's International Data Transfer Agreement, or the UK Addendum to the standard contractual clauses — or must be necessary for a contract with you or for steps you asked us to take before entering one.

The same question applies to FormSubmit, and we have not answered it yet: we do not know which country processes the submissions, so we cannot yet tell you whether a transfer safeguard is needed or which one would apply. Section 03 says what we are doing about that.

07 07 — Cookies

Cookies, analytics and what this site stores

As this notice is published, this website sets no cookies and runs no analytics. We checked the code rather than assuming it.

There is no Google Analytics running on this site, no Google Tag Manager, no Meta or LinkedIn pixel, no advertising tag, no session-recording or heatmap tool and no A/B testing script. No measurement request is sent anywhere, and no cookie is placed in your browser.

That is why you have not been shown a cookie banner. There is nothing on these pages to consent to, and we are not going to ask you to agree to something that is not happening.

Analytics is prepared, but switched off

Google Analytics has been built into the site and then deliberately left inactive: the measurement ID is empty, so Google's script is never requested, no cookie is created and nothing is sent. If that changes, it will not change quietly. Before any analytics can run you will be asked, in a banner, whether you agree — and the code is arranged so that Google's script cannot load until you say yes, rather than loading first and asking afterwards. Refusing will be exactly as easy as agreeing, and if you refuse or simply close the question, nothing is stored and nothing is sent.

You will find a Cookie settings control in the footer of every page. It stays hidden while there is no analytics to configure, and appears by itself the moment any is switched on, so you can change your answer whenever you like. When that happens this section will be rewritten to describe exactly what runs, what it stores and where it goes. Until then, the accurate answer is: nothing.

The fonts come from Google

The pages use three typefaces served by Google Fonts, from fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com. Your browser fetches them directly from Google, which means Google receives your IP address and the fact that a page on this site was loaded. The Google Fonts web API does not set cookies, but that request still happens, and Google is a company based in the United States. We are reviewing whether to serve these font files from our own server so the request stops happening at all.

One page stores something in your browser

One page on this site — the Bupa SEO teardown, at /resources/bupa-seo-teardown/ — works differently from the rest. Its download form goes through FormSubmit as the enquiry forms do, and it also saves two items in your browser's local storage: a flag recording that you unlocked the report, and a copy of the name, email address and phone number you entered. Local storage sits on your own device; you can clear it at any time through your browser settings, usually under "clear site data". That page is under review, and this notice will be updated when it changes.

The server keeps ordinary logs

Like any website, the server that delivers these pages may keep standard access logs — the technical record of an IP address, a browser type and which file was requested. Those are created by our hosting provider as part of running and securing the service, not by us, and we do not use them to identify or follow individual visitors. We are confirming what our host retains and for how long, and this notice will say so.

Links to other sites

The site links out to Google, to WhatsApp and to a small number of other sites. Those links do nothing at all until you click one. Once you do, you are on somebody else's site, under their privacy policy rather than ours.

How to use any of them

Email office@digitalmovement.uk, or write to Digital Movement Marketing Ltd, 128 City Road, London, EC1V 2NX, United Kingdom. You do not need any particular form of words; telling us plainly what you want is enough.
