How much does SEO cost in the UK?

Almost every agency answers this with “it depends” and then asks for your phone number. It does depend — but the things it depends on can be explained, and so can our own price. Here is both.

By Raoul (Alex) Müller, Founder · 28 July 2026

The short answer

UK SEO is priced by the hours of skilled work a campaign needs, so the figure tracks how competitive your market is, how much is broken, and how fast you want to move. Small local campaigns sit at the lower end and competitive national or ecommerce work at the upper end. Digital Movement UK publishes its entry price: Starter SEO from £495 a month, rolling monthly, with no lock-in.

01 01 — What you are buying

There is no price list, because you are buying hours

SEO has no unit. Nobody sells a ranking, because nobody owns one. What an agency actually sells is skilled time — a technical specialist finding what is blocking your pages, a writer producing something worth ranking, someone earning a mention from a publication that will not take money for it, and a senior person deciding which of those to do first.

That makes the pricing question simpler than it looks. A monthly fee is a number of hours. Ask any quote how many, and at what blended rate, and you will learn more in one question than from any proposal deck.

It also explains why the cheap end of the market is so bad. A very low monthly fee cannot buy meaningful hours of a specialist’s time in the UK, so it buys a template report, an automated audit and a handful of directory submissions. That is not SEO done badly; it is not SEO at all.

Two practical notes before the numbers. Check whether a quote includes VAT, because agencies quote both ways. And check what is included beyond the fee — content production, tools, translation, photography and paid placements are sometimes extra.

The one question“How many hours a month does this buy, and who does them?” Any honest agency can answer it.
02 02 — Up and down

What pushes the price up, and what pulls it down

Two businesses in the same town can be quoted three times apart, and both quotes can be fair. These are the reasons.

Pushes it up

A competitive market

If the first page is held by national brands with tens of thousands of referring domains, you need far more content and authority work to compete. Law, insurance, finance, healthcare and anything national are expensive for this reason alone.

Pushes it up

A site with a lot wrong with it

Old platforms, slow templates, broken redirects from a migration, thin pages, duplicated titles, pages nothing links to. Technical debt has to be cleared before anything else works, and clearing it takes weeks of real work.

Pushes it up

Scale and speed

Multiple locations, several languages, a large product catalogue, or a deadline set by a funding round. Wanting results faster does not compress the timeline much, but it does raise the monthly cost of trying.

Pulls it down

A tight geography

One town, one service, few serious competitors. Local campaigns need far fewer hours, which is why a good local programme can be genuinely affordable while a national one cannot.

Pulls it down

A healthy site to start from

A modern platform, clean structure, decent existing content and an in-house person who can implement changes. Every one of those removes hours from the first quarter and moves the budget to work that grows.

Pulls it down

Sharing the work

If your team can write drafts, take photographs, gather reviews or make changes in the CMS, the agency does less and charges less. This is a real lever and most agencies will happily price for it.

The bands overlap because the work overlaps. A £1,200 campaign in a quiet county town can outperform a £3,000 campaign in central London, because the budget is not competing against the same wall of authority. What matters is not the absolute figure but whether it is proportionate to what you are trying to beat.

05 05 — Warning signs

What to be suspicious of in a quote

  • A guaranteed number one ranking. No agency controls Google’s results, and anyone promising a position is either deceiving you or planning to rank you for something nobody searches.
  • A guaranteed citation in ChatGPT or AI Overviews. Those systems are not deterministic and there is no submission route. Probability can be improved; placement cannot be promised.
  • A price quoted before anyone looked at your site or your market. It means the scope was decided by a package, not by your situation.
  • Deliverables counted in units — twenty links a month, ten articles a month. Links that can be ordered by the dozen are the kind search engines discount, and article counts say nothing about whether the pages deserve to rank.
  • A twelve-month minimum with a meaningful exit fee. Long terms exist to protect the agency’s revenue, not your result.
  • Reports that lead with impressions, keyword counts and “work completed”, and never reach enquiries or revenue.
  • The agency owning your accounts. Your website, domain, Google Analytics, Search Console and Business Profile should be in your name, with the agency granted access. Anything else is a hostage arrangement.
  • A secret method. Every real technique in this industry is publicly documented. Secrecy usually conceals either nothing or something you would not sign off on.
06 06 — Judging value

How to tell whether you are getting your money’s worth

Value in SEO is slow to prove, which is exactly why it needs checking early and on the right things.

1 · Before you sign

Ask for the first ninety days in writing

Itemised: what will be audited, what will be fixed, what will be built, who does each piece and roughly how long it takes. A proposal that cannot describe its own first quarter has not thought about yours.

2 · Months 1–3

Judge the work, not the results

Results are not fair to judge yet, but effort is. Ask to see the actual page changes, the technical fixes deployed, the pages published and where any links were placed. If the only evidence is a dashboard, ask again.

3 · Months 3–6

Watch the early indicators

Indexation improving, impressions rising on the terms you care about, target pages entering the top twenty, local grid positions moving. None of these pay the bills yet, but all of them precede the ones that do.

4 · Months 6–12

Move to cost per enquiry

Divide the fee by the qualified enquiries organic search produced, and compare it with what those enquiries cost you elsewhere. Our guide to comparing SEO and paid ads on cost per enquiry sets out the method, and a well-run ads account gives you a fair benchmark to measure against.

5 · Throughout

Ask what you keep if you leave

The content, the technical fixes and the links stay with your site. The accounts should stay in your name. If an agency’s answer to “what happens if we stop” includes anything being taken away, you were renting, not building.
